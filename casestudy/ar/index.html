THE PROBLEM

Knowing what to do wasn't the hard part. Finding where was.

Field engineers upgrade radio base stations alone, often at sites with no signal. Their instructions come as long procedure documents (MOPs). The riskiest moment is cabling: finding one port in a row of near-identical ports, then finding where the cable goes on another unit.

Wrong port terminations Look-alike cables Colour codes vary per customer No connectivity on site
One unit, dozens of similar ports. The guidance has to point at one.
WHERE THE WORK HAPPENS

Rooftops, masts and roadside cabinets.

Every site is set up differently: city rooftops, suburban masts, rural towers shared by several operators. Technicians work outdoors, often alone and without signal, in whatever light and weather they get. The UI had to stay readable over all of it.

VISUAL LANGUAGE

Quiet interface, loud where it counts.

The UI stays neutral and brand-consistent so the camera image and the AR highlights do the talking. Colour is kept for meaning: purple for brand and actions, lime for "this one", yellow for what to target.

WHAT I LEARNED

In AR, the interface gives way to the real world. My job was to make every highlight obvious and everything else calm.

Designing over a live camera image meant testing colour and contrast against real rack photos, not white artboards. Keeping one visual language across the CMS and the app made it clear what the SME creates is what the technician sees. The project was a proof of concept. The material doesn't include measured results, so none are claimed here.
