Case study RBC Express Mobile · 2016

Rebuilding corporate banking around the treasurer’s day

A redesign of RBC Express Mobile — the mobile extension of RBC’s online cash‑management platform — from a text‑heavy list app into a dashboard‑first tool for checking position, approving payments and moving money.

RoleProduct design — UX & UI
UsersRBC corporate clients
PlatformiOS · hybrid (IBM Worklight)
Output24 screens + 1 concept
01 — Context

A capable app that hadn’t changed since launch

RBC Express Mobile lets corporate clients see balances, create transfers, and approve and release payments away from their desks. It launched in July 2014 on a hybrid stack (IBM Worklight, HTML5, jQuery, Cordova) and has since received only maintenance releases — OS certifications, library upgrades and deferred fixes.

Feature coverage was strong. The experience wasn’t: every screen was a dense list of labels and account numbers, and the task clients open the app for — approving payments — sat three levels deep.

05 — Flows · Sign in

Three steps become one

Clients choose how to sign in — User ID and password, or face recognition. The credential screen asks for username and one‑time password together, keeping two‑factor security without the separate token step.

Flows · Approvals

Approving a batch, not an accordion

Approvals get their own tab. A hub shows what’s waiting by type; each list leads with date, parties and amount, and a sticky bar applies Approve, Reject or Hold to everything selected. Hold is new — a way to defer without rejecting.

Before · wire approval Open menu → Approval Manager → expand Wire Payments → tick items → Approve → summary with token password and code → Confirm → confirmation.
Flows · Move money

One form pattern for every kind of payment

Transfers, payments and deposits share a single structure — from, available balance, to, date and amount, note, then Submit / Cancel. The legacy flow had a separate account‑details detour; here the available balance sits right under the source account.

Flows · More

Context around the money

The More tab gathers what a finance team checks alongside its accounts — markets, RBC news, business offers, contacts and the nearest branch — in the same card language as the core screens.

06 — Exploration

A digital‑currency wallet, on the same foundations

A side concept tested how far the system stretches: Touch ID to authorise, a single balance card, and a dated feed of sent, received and transferred amounts with Request, My Account and Send as primary actions.

08 — Reflection

What I’d take into the next round

Settle the fourth tabComps alternate between Approvals and Deposits in slot four. Approvals should win; Deposits belongs in More.
Test bulk approval with approversBatch actions save taps but raise the cost of a mistake; the confirmation and token step needs validating.
Design for French and for AndroidThe app is bilingual and certified on three platforms; labels and the tab bar need longer‑string and Android passes.
Bring releases into the storyRelease Manager (post‑approval) is folded into Approvals conceptually but not yet drawn.
